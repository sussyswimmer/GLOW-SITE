A video interview tests the same abilities as a face-to-face one: clear thinking, honest answers and professional manners. However, the screen adds practical risks that candidates sometimes overlook, and most of them can be removed with preparation.

Start with the technology. Check your internet connection, your camera and your microphone at least a day in advance, and install any software the employer requires. Keep a phone nearby with the meeting link, so that you can rejoin quickly if your connection fails.

Next, consider your surroundings. Sit facing a window or a lamp so that light falls on your face rather than behind you. Place the camera at eye level, and choose a plain, tidy background. Tell the people you live with when the interview begins, to reduce interruptions.

Preparing your content matters even more. Research the organisation, and reread the job description carefully. Many interviewers ask behavioural questions, such as "Tell me about a time you solved a problem." A well-known way to structure these answers is the STAR method: describe the Situation, the Task you were given, the Action you took and the Result. Rehearse out loud, ideally while recording yourself, so that you can notice filler words and a pace that is too fast.

During the interview, look at the camera lens when you speak, since this feels like eye contact to the interviewer. You may keep brief notes beside the screen, but avoid reading from them. If you did not hear a question, ask politely for it to be repeated; this is far better than guessing.

Finally, prepare one or two thoughtful questions for the interviewer, and send a short thank-you message the same day. Careful preparation cannot guarantee an offer, but it lets your abilities, rather than technical problems, speak for you.
